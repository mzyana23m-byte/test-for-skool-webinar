Most physicians do not need to quit. They need a better structure.

You can build locums alongside your current job, learn what your skills are worth, and create options before deciding whether a full transition makes sense.

Dr. Ali Chaudhary will show you how to start the process correctly, avoid the common delays, and make decisions based on your goals rather than pressure from a hospital system.

What we’re covering live

  1. Licensing that does not slow you down

    Find out which states make sense for your goals and whether the Interstate Medical Licensure Compact can help you get licensed faster.

  2. How to know your rate before a recruiter calls

    Learn what affects pay by specialty, region, shift type, and facility. You should know your number before anyone asks you to accept an offer.

  3. What to ask before you let anyone submit your CV

    Understand the questions that reveal whether a job is worth pursuing, including coverage, travel, malpractice, shift expectations, and why the site needs help.

  4. The credentialing system that prevents costly delays

    See the documents, folders, response habits, and workflow that keep credentialing moving. A missed email or missing document can hold up an assignment for weeks.

  5. Building locums without blowing up your current life

    Learn how physicians use locums on the side while keeping a W2 job, then decide when and whether a bigger change makes sense.

  6. The financial setup physicians need to understand

    Get the basic framework for 1099 income, entity structure, business banking, tax planning, benefits, and travel rewards. You will still need advice from qualified legal and tax professionals for your personal situation.

Who this is for

This session is for licensed U.S. attending physicians who:

  • Want more control over when, where, and how much they work.
  • Are considering locums in the next three months.
  • Want to understand rates, licensing, credentialing, and contracts before they make a move.
  • Want an independent career option without having to resign from their current job tomorrow.

This is not for you if

You are still in residency or fellowship, you want someone else to do all the work, or you are not prepared to act on a real opportunity in the next few months.
